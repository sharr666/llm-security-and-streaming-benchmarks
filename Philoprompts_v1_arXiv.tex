\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on sub-word tokenization algorithms---such as Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) and Unigram Language Modeling---alongside safety alignment trained predominantly on high-resource prose~\cite{wei2023jailbroken,yong2023lowresource,boucher2022badcharacters}. This exploratory research note introduces \textbf{Self-Intertwined Deconstructed Philoprompts (Philoprompts-v1.0)}, an experimental multilingual and Unicode test concept authored by Mehrdad Poorhassan (Dr.\ Metal). Each illustrative entry combines three conceptual layers: (1) \textbf{Deconstructed Persian Micro-Poetics and Syntactic Perturbation}, using sub-word morphemic splitting (e.g., \emph{khandidi} $\rightarrow$ \emph{khan didi?}), parenthetical token infilling, and mid-clause punctuation disruption; (2) \textbf{Exploratory Classical and Ancient Script Renderings}, pairing Persian aphorisms with draft encodings across Asokan Brahmi (\texttt{U+11000..U+1107F}), Sumero-Akkadian Cuneiform (\texttt{U+12000..U+123FF}), Old Persian (\texttt{U+103A0..U+103DF}), Avestan (\texttt{U+10B00..U+10B3F}), Classical Chinese (\emph{Wenyanwen}), Ancient Greek, Classical Latin, Classical Ge'ez, and Zargari syllabic cipher (not independently reviewed by specialist philologists); and (3) \textbf{Defensive LLM Security Analysis}, examining how out-of-distribution encodings interact with BPE UTF-8 4-byte fallback pathways and safety classifiers. This note documents an exploratory prototype; independent empirical validation is not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exploratory Corpus Structure and Defensive Scope}

Prior studies demonstrate that LLM safety training can fail under competing objectives and mismatched generalization~\cite{wei2023jailbroken}, translation into low-resource languages~\cite{yong2023lowresource}, and imperceptible character- or Unicode-level perturbations~\cite{boucher2022badcharacters}. Motivated by these failure modes, the exploratory \textbf{Philoprompts-v1.0} corpus is publicly released on Hugging Face~\cite{poorhassan2026hf} and archived under Zenodo DOI \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.24238946}. The release comprises \textbf{563 structured blocks} (\texttt{\#0001} to \texttt{\#0563}, 3.4\,MB in JSON/Parquet format) grouped across conceptual macro-clusters \texttt{C1--C8} plus \texttt{OMEGA}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Schema and Provenance:} Each row specifies \texttt{id} (\texttt{philoprompt-XXXX}), \texttt{block\_id}, \texttt{title}, \texttt{cluster}, \texttt{script}, \texttt{prompt}, \texttt{expected\_behavior} (defensive evaluation target), \texttt{daftar}, \texttt{status}, and persistent identifiers (\texttt{orcid}, \texttt{doi\_repo1}, \texttt{doi\_repo2}).
  \item \textbf{Draft Script Mapping:} Sequential daftars span Brahmi (\texttt{\#0001--\#0010}), Classical Chinese (\texttt{\#0011--\#0020}), Guarani (\texttt{\#0126--\#0140}), Classical Latin (\texttt{\#0141--\#0145}), Ancient Greek (\texttt{\#0149--\#0156}), and subsequent daftars including Sumerian, Sanskrit, Balochi, Ainu, Parthian, and Avestan.
  \item \textbf{Defensive-Only Intended Use:} Released strictly as an exploratory test concept for defensive research on Non-BMP tokenization robustness, prompt-injection resistance, and semiotic deconstruction. It must not be used to bypass safety filters.
\end{itemize}

\takeaway{Archival Note (Author Decision Option C, November 2026)}{Blocks \texttt{\#0146}, \texttt{\#0147}, and \texttt{\#0148}---between Daftar 17 (Latin, ending at \texttt{\#0145}) and Daftar 18 (Ancient Greek, starting at \texttt{\#0149})---are preserved as an intentional silence rather than reconstructed or rewritten. All three records carry \texttt{status = "INTENTIONAL\_GAP"} in the published dataset~\cite{poorhassan2026hf}.}

\section{Companion Prototype: Omega-Stack v2.2 and Limitations}

This exploratory corpus accompanies \textbf{Omega-Stack v2.2} (\texttt{OIAG-285}), a heuristic multi-document RAG quarantine prototype, together with small Python, C++/zlib, Parquet, and POSIX streaming examples~\cite{poorhassan2026repo,poorhassan2026overview}. Incoming chunks require an externally supplied, versioned canonical JSON HMAC signature (\texttt{omega-stack-hmac-v1}) computed over repository, document, chunk, trust-tier, and text fields.

\paragraph{Streaming Examples and Research Status.}
Reference streaming demonstrations on Linux \texttt{x86\_64} (Python 3.11 \texttt{tracemalloc} / RSS) illustrate bounded-memory execution across PyArrow Parquet Snappy ($\approx 3.07\,\mathrm{MB}$ peak heap on 5k batches), C++ \texttt{-O3 -lz} ($\approx 1.80\,\mathrm{MB}$ RSS), and POSIX \texttt{awk+split} ($\approx 2.10\,\mathrm{MB}$ RSS)~\cite{poorhassan2026repo}. Consistent with the companion \texttt{README.md}, Omega-Stack v2.2 and Philoprompts-v1.0 are experimental prototypes and exploratory research notes; independent third-party validation is not claimed.
