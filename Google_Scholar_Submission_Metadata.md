### Abstract (آمادهٔ کپی)

Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on sub-word tokenization algorithms—such as Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) and Unigram Language Modeling—alongside safety alignment trained predominantly on high-resource prose. This exploratory research note introduces Self-Intertwined Deconstructed Philoprompts (Philoprompts-v1.0), an experimental multilingual and Unicode test concept authored by Mehrdad Poorhassan (Dr. Metal). Each illustrative entry combines three conceptual layers: (1) Deconstructed Persian Micro-Poetics & Syntactic Perturbation using sub-word morphemic splitting, parenthetical token infilling, and punctuation boundary disruption; (2) Exploratory Classical & Ancient Script Renderings spanning Asokan Brahmi (U+11000..U+1107F), Sumero-Akkadian Cuneiform (U+12000..U+123FF), Old Persian (U+103A0..U+103DF), Avestan (U+10B00..U+10B3F), Classical Chinese (Wenyanwen), Ancient Greek, Classical Latin, Classical Ge'ez, and Zargari syllabic cipher (draft renderings, not independently reviewed by specialist philologists); and (3) Defensive LLM Security Analysis examining how out-of-distribution encodings interact with BPE UTF-8 4-byte fallback pathways and safety classifiers. This note documents an exploratory prototype; independent empirical validation is not claimed.
